Pairs trading in 60 seconds

Imagine two companies in the exact same business—like two major banks. Because they face identical economic forces, their stock prices usually move in tandem. When unexpected news makes their prices drift apart, pairs trading bets they will eventually snap back together.

1. The Spread

The price difference between two related stocks, scaled by a fitted hedge ratio. When the spread is flat, the market is in equilibrium.

2. The Z-Score

A statistical ruler measuring how rare today's price gap is compared to historical normal, in standard deviations.

3. Enter at 2.0

When the gap widens past 2.0 standard deviations (a top 5% rarity), bet on mean reversion: buy the depressed stock and short the elevated stock.

4. Exit at 0.5

When the gap closes back inside 0.5 standard deviations, close both legs simultaneously to lock in the net profit.

Spread Equation:
Spread_t = ln(Price_A,t) − α − β × ln(Price_B,t)
where α (intercept) and β (hedge ratio) are fitted strictly on training data (2018–2021) via OLS regression.
Z-Score Normalization:
z_t = (Spread_t − μ_train) / σ_train
normalized using the strictly frozen training mean (μ_train) and training standard deviation (σ_train).
Market-Neutral Capital Weights:
w_A = 1 / (1 + |β|),   w_B = −β / (1 + |β|)   (|w_A| + |w_B| = 1.0)
ensuring the dollar exposure on the pair balances out broad market directional moves.
Research Methodology

How I built it

To ensure genuine statistical validity and avoid p-hacking or lookahead bias, this project was developed as a strict, six-stage quantitative research pipeline.

1 Stage 1

Get clean data

Acquired adjusted daily closing prices for 15 large-cap U.S. equities across banks, staples, and energy from 2018 through 2024. Audited for zero splits or missing dates.

2 Stage 2

Find candidate pairs

Formed all 45 unique intra-sector candidate pairs. Constraining pairs to the same industry ensures shared business and economic fundamentals.

src/pairs.py ↗
3 Stage 3

Test cointegration

Ran the Engle-Granger two-step cointegration test on 2018–2021 training prices to verify stationary residuals and estimate mean-reverting half-lives.

src/pairs.py ↗
4 Stage 4

Freeze all settings

Locked all parameters in Day 10: hedge ratios (β), spread means, entry thresholds (±2.0), exit thresholds (±0.5), and max hold days (30). Never re-tuned.

docs/FROZEN_DECISIONS.md ↗
5 Stage 5

Simulate with real costs

Enforced next-day close execution (lag 1) to eliminate impossible same-day fills. Deducted 10 bps per side per leg for commission/slippage, plus 50 bps/year short borrow.

src/backtest.py ↗
6 Stage 6

Test once on locked data

Unlocked the 2023–2024 test period exactly once. No adjustments, no re-fitting, and no second chances—producing authentic out-of-sample performance.

src/portfolio.py ↗

Honesty Checklist

  • Strict Multiple-Testing Honesty: 0 of 45 pairs passed Holm-Bonferroni correction (α = 0.05) in training. The top 5 lowest raw p-values were selected and explicitly labelled exploratory.
  • Untouched Out-of-Sample: 2023 to 2024 was kept completely blind until Day 10 freezing.
  • Execution Delay: Fills occur strictly at the next close (lag 1), eliminating impossible same-day execution.
  • Institutional Friction: Commissions (5 bps), slippage (5 bps), and short borrow fees (50 bps/yr) charged on every trade.

    Honest Empirical Takeaways

    What the results mean

    Rigorous quantitative research means reporting actual findings rather than survivorship-biased marketing. Here is what seven years of testing revealed.

    1. Economic Edge Was Genuine

    The strategy generated positive gross trading profits in all three periods (+$226,590 in Train, +$16,082 in Validation, and +$10,586 in Test). The statistical logic of spread mean-reversion successfully identified mispriced relative value.

    2. Execution Friction Erased the Alpha

    In the untouched 2023–2024 test period, trading friction ($18,199 total) consumed 171.9% of gross trading profits. Because the strategy trades 39 times over two years with a 1-day execution lag, commissions, bid-ask slippage, and borrow costs consumed the entire edge.

    3. Razor-Thin Breakeven Margin

    In the test period, the break-even transaction cost is only 5.12 bps per side. At standard institutional friction (10 bps/side), the strategy loses money. To succeed out-of-sample, execution must be twice as cheap as typical retail costs.

    4. Cointegration Decayed Out-of-Sample

    None of the 45 candidate pairs passed the strict Holm-Bonferroni correction in the training period; the 5 traded pairs were the lowest raw p-values. In 2023–2024, zero of the 5 pairs remained cointegrated, causing persistent non-reverting trends.

    5. Time Stops Protected Against Catastrophe

    The mandatory 30-day maximum holding limit successfully prevented unhedged catastrophic drawdowns when spread relationships broke down (such as COP / DVN during late 2022 and 2023 divergence).

    6. What I Would Try Next

    Future iterations would focus on: (a) Dynamic Kalman Filter hedge ratios to adapt to changing beta, (b) Limit-order passive market making to capture the spread instead of paying it, and (c) Multivariate Johansen cointegration across larger sector baskets.

    Technical Reference

    Glossary

    Hover over highlighted terms throughout the site or review these plain-English definitions of key quantitative finance concepts.

    Spread

    The price gap between two cointegrated stocks, calculated as ln(Price A) − α − β × ln(Price B).

    Z-Score

    How rare today's spread divergence is, expressed in standard deviations from the historical training average.

    Cointegration

    A statistical relationship where two non-stationary price series share a stationary linear combination that does not drift apart indefinitely.

    Hedge Ratio (β)

    The regression slope parameter determining how many shares of Stock B to short per share of Stock A to neutralize market direction.

    Half-Life

    The expected number of trading days required for a price gap dislocation to revert halfway back to its historical mean.

    Basis Point (bps)

    One-hundredth of one percent (0.01% or 0.0001). 10 basis points equals 0.10%.

    Long Spread

    A position betting the price gap will widen upward: buying Stock A and shorting Stock B.

    Short Spread

    A position betting the price gap will revert downward: selling Stock A and buying Stock B.

    Sharpe Ratio

    A measure of risk-adjusted return calculating annualized return divided by annualized return volatility.

    Drawdown

    The peak-to-trough decline in total portfolio capital during an adverse market streak.

    Slippage

    The execution penalty between the expected price when an order is decided and the actual fill price.

    Borrow Cost

    The annual financing fee paid to brokers to borrow shares for short sales (modeled as 50 bps/year).

    Overfitting

    Fitting statistical parameters to historical noise rather than genuine recurring economic equilibrium.

    Out-of-Sample

    A chronological time window (2023–2024) untouched during model development to test authentic predictive performance.
